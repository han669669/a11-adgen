# Creative playbook

Read this file before you write ideas for a batch (`ideas_save`). It replaces the four weekly formats with three funnel stages (owner, 07-11-2026). The owner's taste notes in `docs/taste-seed.md` still apply. This file says how to use them. The owner's picture notes from v1 (writing on props, scale, props, physics, hands) are in `docs/render-checklist.md`.

Sources: the owner's "$100M Static Ad Playbook" (pasted in chat, 07-11-2026), the W46 drafts, option B (`docs/p0-wongi-final-b.jpg`), and Meta Ad Library research for Singapore (Apify runs `rIMpAosXb7tdOEvVl` and `vSy2L8RrlzMeyeT2w`, 07-11-2026).

## 1. Why W46 failed and option B worked

The 12 W46 drafts had no render defects. The jar, the label and every word were correct. The owner still found none of them usable. Option B was usable.

| | W46 drafts | Option B |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | A product fact or a logistics line ("What's in every crunch.", "Four jars. Free delivery.") | A buyer's own feeling ("Somehow the jar is empty again.") |
| Picture | Illustrates the headline with props (suitcase, chip bag, callout lines) | Is the joke: the jar is almost empty |
| Variety | 12 drafts, one look: beige wall, jar in the centre, headline on top | One calm, warm product photo |
| Pick | One idea per format, so a weak format idea beat a strong idea | It was the strongest idea. W46 left it out because another "hook" was picked. |
| Critic | Ranked craft (jar centred, label clean) | (not ranked by the critic) |

The cause was the idea and the pick, not the render. The model drew every W46 layout cleanly. Do not add render rules to fix an idea problem (`docs/v1-lessons.md` §2.2).

## 2. The bar: the option B test

Pick an idea only when the answer to all four questions is yes:

1. **One idea.** Do the picture and the headline make one point together? Cover the headline: does the picture still carry half of it?
2. **The buyer's truth.** Does a Singaporean snacker see their own behaviour or feeling in it, in words they would use? Not a product claim.
3. **Calm.** One focal product. Does every prop, line and sticker earn its place (`taste-seed.md` §1, "Hates"; `render-checklist.md` F2, F6)?
4. **Real.** Is the product the real packshot (draft from the kit's packshot reference), with the real logo drawn in code? If the ad shows food, does the food match the kit's texture photos (§6)? Food that looks painted rejects the ad.

Then apply the owner gate in `taste-seed.md` §2 (Singapore truth, no ethnicity, race or religion, logo, funnel fit), then the render defects.

## 3. Funnel stages

Three ads per batch: 1 tof, 1 mof, 1 bof (owner, 07-11-2026). The database allows at most one pick per stage, and `batch_update` requires exactly one per stage before status `board`. An engine 3 batch (a11-engine v3, owner 16-11-2026) differs: the critic selects the best 3 concepts from any stage and gives each a role, so two picks can have one role; the database allows 3 picks per batch, and `batch_update` requires exactly 3 before status `board`. The owner runs batches until one ad is top tier, so judge each final as if it is the only ad the client sees.

| Stage | Job | What to lead with | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tof` top of funnel (cold) | Stop a stranger who has not heard of the brand. | A human truth or a persona sorting line ("For the one who finishes the jar in one sitting"). Native looks are allowed: a meme layout, a phone-photo moment. Never a handwritten note or sticky note: painted handwriting reads as AI (`render-checklist.md` D1). | Offer stickers, prices, feature lists. |
| `mof` middle (warm) | Give a reason to switch. | Sell the switch: name what they eat now (fried chips, the office biscuit tin, the 3 pm vending machine) and the one difference that matters. Or credibility from a figure this buyer trusts (an archetype, never a real person without consent). | Callout lists of every feature. |
| `bof` bottom (ready to buy) | Give a reason to buy now. | One simple, true offer from the product facts (for example a delivery threshold, a bundle, a guarantee), with its source. Show the product clearly. | A freebie stack the brand cannot fund. A wrong price. |

## 4. What works for Singapore snack brands (research, 07-11-2026)

- The proven angle is "you cannot stop eating it". IRVINS runs "Dangerously Addictive". The Cocoa Trees runs "bold, addictive flavours that are hard to put down" in 4 versions since 08-09-2026 (the longest-running snack ad found). The Purest writes "3AM: 'Maybe just one chip' (it's never one chip)". Option B uses the same truth without the word "addictive". Find new ways into this truth. Do not copy their words.
- Most Singapore food ads are plain food photos. A clear idea stands out because the bar is low.
- Native voice gets spend: "Lowkey obsessed with this" (49 Seats), first-person stories (most long-running non-food ads in the sample).

## 5. Process for each batch

1. Research: the product page facts and buyer words (Reddit, reviews). Skip the Ad Library through Apify (the oldest active statics in the category, and how long each has run) until its Phase 4 integration is built and the owner approves the paid step. Read the angle, never the look.
2. Burst: at least 8 ideas per stage before you pick. Ideas cost nothing; drafts cost money.
3. Kill: `ideas_save` the whole burst with no idea picked. The critic subagent picks, in a fresh context with only the batch id (it reads the ideas with `batch_get` and saves the 3 picks with `ideas_pick`): it applies §2, and kills an idea that needs a `render-checklist.md` defect (D1 to D9) to work. Most ideas must die here. If fewer than the needed picks survive, write more ideas, save them with `ideas_save` and give the batch id to the critic again before any draft. Do not lower the bar.
4. Looks: give each of the 3 picks its own look (§7.2): a different backdrop, light and camera per stage. Do not copy option B's look onto every ad.
5. Draft: 3 `low` drafts per picked idea, one render each (`numImages` 1). The art-director subagent writes the draft prompts and the skeleton placements, and checks each finished draft (shape, food, words, `render-checklist.md`) before the rank; the main agent runs the renders. A redraft after a full pass goes through the same two steps. Each draft starts from its own product-lock skeleton (§7.4): the real product at its exact proportions, placed differently per draft (position, scale, crop, light, camera), so the 3 drafts differ and the product shape is exact. When the packshot cannot show the idea's product state (an open, nearly empty jar), draft with the packshot only (§7.1, prompt 1) and build the product's state from an open source (§7.4 C) and swap it into the picked draft.
6. Review (the owner picks, 07-11-2026): the critic subagent ranks the drafts blind in a fresh context with only the batch id. It reads `taste_log` first, then saves the rank with `drafts_rank` (it judges each against §2, then the product shape against the packshot §7.3, then `render-checklist.md` §1 to §3 at phone size, craft last; writes what it sees in each reason). The main agent does not give it a view of the drafts and does not read the rank before the owner picks. Then STOP. Show the owner the drafts: one picture per idea with its 3 drafts numbered 1, 2, 3 from left to right in image order, and no rank. Name any draft that has a D item and tell him not to pick it. Do not say which you prefer. The owner picks 1 eligible draft per idea. If he passes on all 3 drafts of an idea, save his words with `batch_update` `ownerVerdicts` (stage `draft`, mark `pass`, `ideaId`). Then redraft that idea (step 5), rank the new drafts blind, show them as above and wait again: that idea goes on to `picks_save` only when he picks one of the new drafts. Later, when the final is made and shown to him, save each note on it the same way (stage `final`, the final picture's `key`, mark `fix`, `approve` or `reject`), and in the pack step, when the copy is shown to him, each verdict on it (stage `pack`, mark `fix`, `approve` or `reject`, `packVersion` when that copy is already published), with his exact words; `taste_log` returns them for `learn`. Save his picks with `picks_save` and his own words. Read the result: where he chose a draft you ranked lower, find what he saw that you missed and let it steer the next batch. Then the final (§7.1, prompt 2) and, when food shows, the finish pass (§7.1, prompt 3).
7. Poll each final, finish pass and repair with `render_status`. The art-director subagent checks each final: check the resulting key against §2 and §7.3, run the food check (§6.4) when food shows, and inspect any repair and its edges at full size. `food_fix` and `region_fix` are repair tools for a rare defect on an otherwise good final, not default steps. When a final needs more than one repair, redraft the idea with a simpler scene instead. Skip the finish pass when the real product pixels carry the food (§7.4): in W46c v2 the finish pass turned real kernels into glossy, round, spotted ones. Then `compose_final` only the verified completed key.
8. Final check: §2 and `render-checklist.md` again on the composed final. When a final fails, say which item in the report. Do not send it.
9. A newer version for a link the client already has (owner, 09-11-2026): publish the newer version with `pack_publish` and show it to the owner. When he approves it and names the older link to change, run `pack_update` (`version` = the older pack, `fromVersion` = the approved one). The older link keeps its ad names, the client's comments and uploads, and its own approved Meta text, fact check and launch plan; only the pictures change. Before each `pack_update`, ask the owner: pictures only (the default), or the new copy as well? Give `text: true` only when he chose the new copy, after he saw the old and new copy side by side (owner, 09-11-2026). Never revoke a link to do this. `pack_update` with `undo` puts the old content back.

## 6. Real food (owner, 07-11-2026)

The food must look like the real product. In W46b every final showed the caramel Pop Barley as round balls with leopard spots. The real kernels are lumpy, split open, with a crackled caramel net. A one-line prompt instruction did not fix it reliably, so food has its own stage.

1. **Kit.** Each food brand's kit has texture photos (real close-ups) and `food`: the real food in words, written once from those photos. Say the shape, the surface, the colour, how the pieces sit, and what it must never look like. Save it with `brand_save`. Name the colour against its wrong neighbour ("deep amber, darker than pale tan") and the pattern's shape ("angular cracks, not round dots"), and name small true details (a split seam with a dark husk line). In W46b the general words gave pale kernels with leopard dots; these exact words gave the real colour and cracks in one round (kit v3).
2. **Drafts and finish.** Closed-product drafts use the product-lock skeleton as the only reference (§7.4); when the packshot cannot show the product state, draft with the packshot only (§7.1). The texture photo and the food words go into the finish pass (prompt 3), except when the real product pixels are in the picture (§7.4): then skip the finish pass. Show as little loose food as the idea allows: food behind the pack's own window reads as real; a heap in close-up shows every AI flaw.
3. **`food_fix` (repair only).** When the finish pass (§7.1, prompt 3) still shows food that fails the food check, give boxes around all the food (also the food inside a jar). The server builds a mask. fal treats the mask as a guide only and re-renders the whole picture (in W46b the chips outside the mask became barley). So when the result arrives, the Worker pastes only the boxes onto the original final: outside the boxes the picture stays the original (re-encoded once as JPEG at quality 95). When the paste cannot run, the render fails; the raw fal image is never saved. Text, glass, reflections, labels or props inside a box can still change, so keep the boxes tight and check those parts in the food check. In `keep`, never call the surface under the food "clean" or "empty": in W46b that removed the heap. The references are the final, the texture photos and the packshot. The texture photo is a texture cue only: the amount of food and its state (heap, jar fill level, crumbs) come from the final, not from the photo.
4. **Food check.** Crop the food at full size and put it next to the texture photo. Fail it when you see repeated identical shapes, perfect spheres, a dot or spot pattern, gloss the photo does not show, the wrong colour, or a painted look. Also fail it when a text, label, glass edge or prop inside a box changed, or when food inside a box went missing or moved. On a fail, run `food_fix` again on the new key, with tighter boxes or more `keep` detail. Never pass food you did not compare.
5. **Compose.** Run `compose_final` only on the version that passed the food check.

## 7. The house recipe (owner, 07-11-2026)

Option B was made in 3 renders plus the logo in code. W46b used more steps and busier scenes, and every extra prop, reference or stage added a new defect. On 07-11-2026 the same recipe, run again through the current tools, gave an ad almost the same as option B, with the correct label (US$0.19; `docs/decisions.md`). So this recipe is the standard. The owner: "different look for each funnel stage, no anchoring".

### 7.1 The three prompts

Fill the parts in angle brackets. Keep the rest of the words.

1. **Draft** (fallback only, for a product state the packshot cannot show, §5 step 5; the normal draft is one render per draft from a product-lock skeleton, §7.4) (`render_submit` kind `draft`, 3 images, low; references: the packshot only):
   > <Look opener: "Premium studio product photograph" for a studio look, "Real phone photo, honest and unposed" for a native look> for a static social ad. Image 1 is the exact product: <product name>. Keep its label, <the label's main elements>, <the cap or closure> and <the pack material> exactly as in the photo. <The kit's shape words (kit notes), with the product's real size.>
   >
   > Scene: <one or two sentences: where the product stands (which side of the frame, not centred unless the look says so), its state that carries the idea (open, almost empty, one missing), at most one other object at its true size>. <This stage's backdrop, light and camera (§7.2)>. No other objects. No hands.
   >
   > No words, letters or numbers anywhere in the picture except the product's own print. Leave the top band of the picture plain for a logo added later, and leave <the headline area, for example the upper right 45% of the width from 18% to 45% of the height> as plain, even <backdrop> for words added later.
   >
   > Calm and minimal, with generous clean space. <The `render-checklist.md` §4 style line for this look.> Not an illustration, no glossy AI look.

2. **Final** (kind `final`, high; references: the chosen draft only):
   > Recreate image 1 as a high-quality final ad. Keep everything in image 1 the same: composition, camera, light, the product, its label <with its distinctive details, for example "the dark brown bands at its top and bottom">, and the product's own print exactly. Do not add any word, letter or number. Keep the top band and <the headline area> plain and even, with nothing in them (the words are set in code after this render, §7.6).
   >
   > The story of the ad depends on this, so keep it exactly as in image 1: <the product state and every object, in words>.
   >
   > Keep the light, the shadows and the backdrop exactly as in image 1. Improve only the finish: crisp clean letter edges, real <material> reflections and the natural texture of this look (for example fine grain on a studio sweep, phone grain on a native photo). A real photograph, calm and minimal, no glossy AI look.

3. **Finish pass**, only when food shows (kind `final`, high; references: the final, then the kit's texture photo):
   > Recreate image 1 as the same high-quality ad with exactly one change. Keep everything else identical: <the same list as prompt 2>.
   >
   > The change: the <food> must look like real photographed food, exactly like image 2 (a real close-up photo of this product): <the kit's food words>. Keep the same amount in the same places.
   >
   > A real product photograph, calm and minimal.

Then `compose_final` with `brandSlug`.

### 7.2 One look per stage

Pick a different look for each of the 3 ads in a batch, and a different set from the last batch where you can. A look is a backdrop, a light and a camera. Write it in one sentence in prompt 1. Examples, not a fixed list:

| Look | Backdrop | Light | Camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm studio (option B) | seamless warm cream sweep | soft window light from the left, leaf or window shadows | eye level, product a little right of centre |
| Colour block | one bold kit colour, flat | hard direct light, one crisp shadow | slightly above, product centred low |
| Real surface | a real material from the buyer's day (stone counter, wooden desk) | morning daylight | three-quarter view from above |
| Phone photo (native) | an ordinary real place | mixed indoor light, slight phone grain | handheld phone height, slightly tilted |
| Top-down | one plain surface | even soft light | straight down |

### 7.3 Product truth checks before you pick

- **Shape.** Compare each draft with the packshot: height to width, cap width and height, label position, every band and colour block on the label. Measure it, do not judge it by eye: read the product's box in each draft from a grid overlay and compare the height to width with the packshot's. Real WonGi jar (cap top to base): 2.65. The drafts the owner picked in W46c (07-11-2026, grid reading, about ±1%): mof 2.46 (9% too squat, cap 0.28 of the width tall against 0.40 real), bof 2.76 (4% too slim, cap 0.35). The owner saw both as wrong, and I had called them right by eye. Shape words in the prompt did not fix it. The product lock (§7.4) does. Where a draft has no lock, drop a draft that is more than 3% off.
- **One object at most** next to the product, and only when the idea needs it (`render-checklist.md` F2).
- **Reference state.** A reference carries its state (Phase 0, lesson 2). Never pass a reference whose state differs from the scene, except the texture photo in prompt 3.

### 7.4 Product lock (owner, 07-11-2026)

**Why.** A model redraws the product from a photo and drifts: in W46c the jar came out 9% too squat with a short cap, and no wording in the prompt fixed it (a two-reference prompt that asked for the packshot's proportions changed nothing). A model does keep what is already in its input image. So the real product, cut from the packshot, goes into the input at the exact proportions, and the model builds the scene around it. The label, the kernels and the shape are then real pixels. `scripts/product_lock.py` does this (Python with Pillow and numpy, run in the session).

**Packshot boxes** (read once per product from a grid overlay of the packshot; the measurements are in the kit notes or here):

| Product | Packshot | `--box` (x0,y0,x1,y1) | `--band-top` | Height ÷ width |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WonGi Caramel Pop Barley jar | 1024 x 1024, cream backdrop | 340,70,686,986 | 286 | 2.65 |
| Same jar, cap off (open source, §7.4 C) | 1024 x 1280 padded | 340,216,686,1114 | n/a | 2.58 (derived, see C) |

**0. Food grade (food products).** The real packshot kernels are true but pale and flat, and the owner found food built from them "not appetizing" (W46c v2). Colour-grade the packshot toward the real close-up of the same food before any lock: `python3 scripts/food_grade.py --src <packshot> --ref <close-up> --ref-box <a patch of kernels> --region <food area> [--region <another>] --out <graded packshot>`. A Lab mean and spread transfer changes the kernels' colour (warm golden amber, from the real close-up), their lightness and their contrast, and clarity adds local detail back; a mask keeps the label, glass and backdrop, so the real texture and shape stay. Verify the graded result next to the close-up (§6.4) before it becomes the `--packshot`. A model repaint of the food, tried twice, gave round, glossy, spotted kernels. Pale kernels can fall under the kernel mask (the script then stops with a message): lower `--sat-min` and `--dist`. Use the graded file as `--packshot` below. The defaults are the softer W46c v3 settings (strength 0.8, no spread, contrast or clarity boost): the owner found them "slightly more appetizing" than the ungraded packshot. The stronger v4 settings (strength 1, spread x1.15, contrast x1.12, clarity 0.7) looked "fake, plastic" to him, so they are options only (`--sd-boost`, `--contrast`, `--clarity`). Check the result next to the close-up (§6.4). Do not follow it with a model finish pass: tried again on v4 with food words that copy the close-up, the model drew round, patchy, glossy kernels (the look of `docs/p0-wongi-final-b.jpg`) and the owner had just called that look AI food.

**A. Skeleton drafts (default for a closed product).**

1. Per draft, make a skeleton from a plan file: `python3 scripts/product_lock.py layout --packshot <file> --box <box> --plan <plan.json> --out <file>`, and keep the plan next to the skeleton as `<skeleton name>.plan.json`: `reprint` needs the plan of the picked draft (steps 5 and 6), so note which plan each draft was made from. The plan gives the canvas, the backdrop colour, flat `shapes` that draw the room's real-scale cues, and each product's `cx`, `base`, `height` (fractions of the canvas), `side` (`up`, or `L`/`R` for a carton lying on its side; `R` reads left to right) and a small `rot` in degrees (see `load_plan` in the script). One product is a plan with one entry. For a group, never place identical copies at equal gaps in one row: give each a turn of 0.5 to 3 degrees, uneven gaps, some lying, one leaning or upright (owner, W47 Oatside, 16-11-2026: "6 of the product placed in exact width apart and exactly the same looking it screams ai image"). For true scale, set the product height from its real size and draw cues of known size as shapes at the same px per cm: subway tile rows 7.5 cm (at about 0.85 of that scale on a wall 20 cm behind), a 4 cm worktop edge (W47 bof: the same stack with no cues read as the wrong size; with the drawn tiles and worktop the owner approved it). The older `skeleton --count 4` row stays only for one product or a planned even row. Give each of the 3 drafts its own placement. Examples that worked for one idea: centred at 57% of the height, eye level, light from the left; large (74%) and right of centre with the headline on the left, camera slightly low; small (44%) at the left with a lot of empty space, camera from above, light from the right. Keep the headline's room clear in the layout. For a product that is not a box in a bottle or jar (for example a cake on a box), first cut the product out with `python3 scripts/product_lock.py cutout --packshot <file> --out <alpha.png> --dark-max 40 --smooth 2.0 --open 7 --erode 3`, check the mask on a flat colour at full size (a dark textured part such as a brownie on a black backdrop must be whole; a plain `--dark-max` mask cuts it, which the owner saw as a hole in the cake), then pass the same `--alpha <alpha.png>` to `layout` and to `reprint` (the older `skeleton` and `swap` take it too). Clear any one-pixel hairline the mask leaves by hand. Cat & the Fiddle (07-11-2026): box `0,121,599,660`; the cake bleeds off the left edge, so put the headline on the right. The packshot cuts the box's left end: place each skeleton so that this end sits at or past the left frame edge (§7.5, owner 12-11-2026): on the 1024 px canvas, `--cx` at most the product width ÷ 2048 (`skeleton` prints the width; at `--jar-height 0.57` the width is about 840 px, so `--cx` 0.41 or less, not the 0.5 above). For a range of different items (each flavour of a cake as a slice), each plan product names its own real photo: `"photo"`, its mask `"alpha"` (from `cutout` on that photo; a white studio backdrop works at the defaults) and `"blur"` in px for an item further back; then `layout` needs no `--packshot` (owner, 18-11-2026: model-drawn slices were "ai food slop"; the brand's real slice photos composed this way were kept by the model, with the prompt "keep every slice exactly"). `reprint` does not take such a plan. Use the real packshot as it is: never edit its pixels (owner, 12-11-2026: the box's real fold line at its corner must stay, "matching of the reference image supplied from the brand's product"; the W46 rerun lost it because a patch removed it before the skeletons).
2. Upload it: `upload_link` (kind `ref`, `ext` `jpg` or `png`), then run the `curl` in its answer with the file. The answer gives the `/m/refs/<uuid>` url that fal reads. No admin token is needed. (`PUT /admin/ref/<uuid>.jpg` with the bearer token still works for the owner.)
3. `render_submit` kind `draft`, `numImages` 1, the skeleton as the only reference, this prompt (fill the brackets, add the variation line for that draft):
   > This image is a layout sketch: the product on the plain canvas is the real product, already placed at its true proportions. Turn the sketch into one <look opener> for a static social ad. Keep the product exactly as it is: its shape, height, width, cap, label and contents, and its size and position in the frame. Do not redraw or reshape it. Add around it: <this stage's backdrop and surface>, <light>, a crisp soft-edged shadow, a small contact shadow under the product, natural clear-plastic reflections, and clear plastic that shows the backdrop behind it (not the cream of the packshot). No other objects. No hands.
   >
   > No words, letters or numbers anywhere in the picture except the product's own print. Leave the top band of the picture plain for a logo added later, and leave <the headline area> as plain, even <backdrop> for words added later.
   >
   > <Variation line: camera, light, scale, where the product stands.> <The `render-checklist.md` §4 style line.>
4. The 3 drafts now differ in layout, scale, camera and light, so the owner's pick teaches more. The skeleton fixes the shape in the model's input, not in its output: measure each draft and each final (§7.3) and drop or swap any that is more than 3% off.
5. Real print, real-camera finish, then words and print on each draft, before the owner sees it (free, local): first `python3 scripts/product_lock.py reprint --packshot <file> --box <box> --face <printed face box> --plan <the skeleton's plan> --render <raw render> --out <file>` (§7.4 F), then `python3 scripts/photo_finish.py --in <draft-reprinted> --out <draft-finished> --seed <n>` (§7.7), then `python3 scripts/headline.py --in <draft-finished> --out <draft-text> --family "<brand primary font, or its next site font when the primary is the logo's face (§7.6); a font file the site serves goes in --font and --sub-font>" --wght <heaviest weight> --sub-wght <one step lighter> --sub-ratio 0.40 --gap 0.30 --case upper --leading 1.0 --logo <kit logo PNG> --logo-width <kit logoWidthPct / 100> --logo-top <kit logoTopPct / 100> --headline "<approved headline, with | at the line breaks>" --subline "<approved subline>" --area <the empty side as fractions> --color <kit text colour> [--align center] [--accent "<key words>" --accent-color <calm accent, §7.6 Accent>]` (§7.6), then for a boxed product `scripts/box_print.py` (§7.5) on the draft with the words, so the box print is the real ink (checked 12-11-2026 on 4 low W46 drafts at 1024x1280: each matched, and the smeared "artisanally conceived" came out crisp; if the script stops, drop or redraw the draft). Show the owner these pictures on a sheet: the board shows the text-free renders. Drop or repair a draft with a stray mark, a smeared line or a moved box corner (W46 rerun: a dot above a letter, smeared "artisanally conceived"); never show the owner a draft with a known defect.
6. Final (§7.1, prompt 2) from the picked draft (the raw render, not the finished one) as before, then `compose_final` with `grain` "off" (it registers the final), then build the picture in code from the raw final (§7.7): `product_lock.py reprint` with the picked draft's plan (§7.4 F), then `photo_finish.py`, then `headline.py` with `--logo <kit logo PNG> --logo-width <kit logoWidthPct / 100> --logo-top <kit logoTopPct / 100>` (add `--logo-x <left edge>` so the logo heads the text column; keep the width and top flags) and the same words and area, measured again against the logo, then `box_print.py` for a boxed product, then `upload_link` kind repair with the result, so `picks_get` and the pack take the picture with the words. Until that repair, the registered final has the logo only: do not run `pack_publish` until `picks_get` shows `repaired: true` on each final built in code. No render finish pass (§7.1, prompt 3).

**B. Swap (repair of a picked draft or final that has a wrong shape, or a product state the skeleton cannot show).**

1. Read the old product's box in the picked draft from a grid overlay: `x0,x1,top,base`.
2. `python3 scripts/product_lock.py swap --packshot <file> --box <box> --scene <draft> --old <x0,x1,top,base> --out <file>`. For an open, nearly empty jar add `--lower-only --band-top <y>`: the label and everything below it become real packshot pixels, and the open neck and rim stay from the draft. ### 7.6 Words in the brand's own font (owner, 12-11-2026)

The image model cannot draw a given font: it paints a look-alike (W46 rerun: "it doesn't use the brand's own font type, we should be using whatever fonts they are using when we were building up the brand kit"). So no prompt asks for words, and `scripts/headline.py` sets the approved headline and subline in the kit's font on each draft and on each composed final. It works for every brand whose copy is in Latin script: `--family` takes the kit's font name (`brand_get` `kit.fonts`; the first is the headline font unless the kit notes say otherwise; a second kit font can set the subline with `--sub-family`), and the script gets that family at the weight from Google Fonts and keeps the file in a local cache. The site's own weights (Cat & the Fiddle: Oswald SemiBold 600, Light 300 for the subline) were too light for an ad: use the display style below. A brand font that is not on Google Fonts (a licensed or custom font): download the file the brand's own site serves (its `@font-face` url) and give it with `--font`. When neither is possible, `--fallback` names the nearest Google Fonts family of the same kind (condensed sans, serif, script); the script prints FALLBACK, and the owner is told before he judges the type. `--area` is the headline area that the prompt kept plain, as fractions of the width and height; the script finds the largest size that fits (at most 3 headline lines, 2 subline lines) and prints the text's top and bottom, so the logo gap can be checked (the text top at 18% of the height or lower). Checked 12-11-2026 on a W46 skeleton with Oswald 600/300, Martel 700 with Assistant 400, and a missing family with and without a fallback. Right-to-left, CJK and other complex scripts are refused with a clear message (wrapping is by spaces and Pillow gets no shaping), so such copy is set another way and the owner is told.

**The display style (owner, 15-11-2026).** The kit's web font at its site weight, in sentence case on one long line, failed: on the W46 Sonnet drafts (Oswald SemiBold) the owner said the type was "simply really really ugly and ill fitting", and he pointed to the W47 v3 pack as the standard. The v3 words were painted by the model before §7.6; they are a heavy, narrow poster face (the nearest Google font is Anton; Oswald Bold in capitals is next). The owner chose the brand's own font, styled well: "always be using what the brand's primarily using, as a font type typography has several options to deliver better results" (15-11-2026, Oswald Bold in capitals for Cat & the Fiddle, not Anton). Set every brand's words in this style:

- **Not the logo's face.** When the brand's heading font is the face of its logo (IRVINS: CF Jacques Parizeau), the headline "feels blended" with the logo (owner, 16-11-2026). Then set the words in the brand's other site font (IRVINS: Museo Sans Rounded 800 caps, 700 subline).
- **Face.** Always the brand's primary font (the first of `kit.fonts`), except when it is the logo's face (the rule above: then the brand's next site font), at its heaviest weight for the headline (Cat & the Fiddle: Oswald 700) and one step lighter for the subline (Oswald 600), never Light. Do not swap in another family; get the poster look from case, weight, leading and layout. Only when the brand font cannot be had at all does `--fallback` apply, and the owner is told.
- **Headline.** `--case upper`, the heaviest weight, `--leading 0.97` to `1.0` (1.0 for Oswald), 2 or 3 short lines. The script skips a size whose wrap leaves one short word alone on a line ("FOR") when a smaller size avoids it. Put ` | ` where a line should end on the idea ("Choosing | is the | hard part.", "Can’t pick | a flavour?"); the words stay the approved words.
- **Place.** In the empty side next to the product, as in v3: a right column (`--area 0.50,0.18,0.95,0.42` when the product sits lower left and its top at the column's left edge is at 45% of the height or lower), or centred above a large product (`--align center --area 0.08,0.165,0.92,0.36`). Measure the product's top first; leave a gap of at least 3% above it (4% by default; `headline.py` refuses less than 3%: owner, 20-11-2026).
- **Subline.** `--sub-ratio 0.40 --gap 0.30`, sentence case (v3 used both cases; sentence case reads friendlier under capitals).
- **Accent.** One key phrase, on one ad of the three at most (v3: "TEN?" on the tof ad only), in a calm version of the kit's accent colour: the full kit red #FA363A was "too strikingly in the face ... very distracting" (owner, 15-11-2026); the v3 red, sampled from the ad, is #DD4430: `--accent "HARD PART." --accent-color DD4430`. For another brand, take its accent colour about 10% darker and less saturated.
- **Logo.** The owner judges the drafts as whole ads, so each draft shows the kit logo at the top centre as a preview: `headline.py --logo <kit logo PNG> --logo-width <kit logo width> --logo-top <kit logo top>`, as fractions, from the kit placement (`brand_get` `kit.placement.logoWidthPct` and `logoTopPct`, the same placement `compose_final` uses; Cat & the Fiddle: 0.24 and 0.05). The script default (width 25%, top 4.5%) is only for a kit with no placement. The script stops before it draws when the logo reaches the text. On finals give the same `--logo`: the picture is built in code after `compose_final` with grain off (§7.7), so the script draws the real logo there. Keep the text top under the logo (18% of the height in a column, 16.5% when centred).

- **Breathing space and one logo size (owner, 20-11-2026).** On the Allies of Skin test the logo was 20% of the width on one ad and 28% on another, and the words sat close to the logo and the product: "u kept on messing up the brand company logo sizing and the headline and subline's empty space ... all the empty space needing breathing space". So:
  - One logo size and place for each brand, on every ad: `--logo-width` and `--logo-top` from the kit placement, never chosen per ad. When the kit's top is under 4%, save the kit with a top of 4% or more first (`brand_save`). Allies of Skin: width 0.28, top 0.04, `--logo-x 0.07` at the head of the text column.
  - The headline starts at the same height on every ad of a batch (Allies: 10.5% of the height), and its size stays close from ad to ad (Allies: 6.9% to 7.3% of the height).
  - `headline.py` refuses a picture when a space is too small: the text area or the logo less than 4% from a side or the top, or the words less than 4% above the bottom edge (`--min-margin`), less than 3% between the logo bottom and the text top (`--min-logo-gap`), or less than 3% between the words and the product (`--min-clear`, default 4%).
  - The claim's footnote is set by the script too: `--footnote "<study line>" [--footnote-align right] [--footnote-y 0.945]`, at 1.75% of the height, 80% opacity. Its words must be Latin script, as the headline. The script refuses a footnote over an object or a shadow edge (the mirror ad: the handle covered "analysis"). Try the other side or move it down; it may end 2.5% above the bottom edge.
- **One tone under the words (owner, 21-11-2026).** On the Cat & the Fiddle engine 3 test the headline crossed the edge of a white and brand-yellow two-block stage, so the split cut "Chocolate" and "so". `headline.py` refuses a picture when a sharp colour or shadow edge runs straight down through a line of words, or across the words (between the lines too). A smooth light fall-off and the photo grain pass. On a two-block stage put the whole text column on one block, or ask the render for one plain tone under the words (the remade final had one white band over the top 41%).

Checked 15-11-2026 on the 9 W46 Sonnet drafts (batch f7886f1d) in Oswald 700/600: each set in 2 or 3 lines with no stub line, text top 16.5 to 18% and bottom 35.0 to 42.0% of the height, clear of the product.

### 7.7 Real-camera finish (owner, 15-11-2026; the standard from 16-11-2026)

The owner liked the light film grain on the W46 Sonnet finals and asked for more of a real camera's traits, so that no viewer thinks "AI image" at first glance. A raw render is too clean: no grain, the same crisp edge everywhere, clipped digital white, colour a little too saturated and no lens traits. `scripts/photo_finish.py` adds those traits back in code, in a colourist's order: tone (a soft highlight shoulder, a small black lift, a trim of only the most saturated colours), a light soften (removes over-sharpened edges), a faint warm halation round the brightest highlights, lens traits (a soft vignette, a lateral colour fringe under 1 px, corners a little softer than the centre) and organic grain last (clumped, strongest in the mid-tones, mostly luminance). Grain goes last so no other step smears it, and the words go on after it so they stay crisp, as type set in post on a real shoot. On a flat brand-colour backdrop, pass `--keep <brand colour as RRGGBB>` (owner, 18-11-2026: the tone step turned the Fickle Feline yellow olive, because its highlight shoulder pulls red down more than green): pixels near that colour skip the tone and halation steps, and the other steps still run.

- **Drafts.** Run it on each raw draft after `product_lock.py reprint` (§7.4 F) and before `headline.py`; give a fixed `--seed` so a rerun gives the same grain.
- **Finals.** `compose_final` with `grain` "off" (it registers the final), then build the picture from the raw final: `product_lock.py reprint` (§7.4 F), the script, then `headline.py` with `--logo` and the words, so the logo and the words go on after the finish and stay crisp, as type set in post on a real shoot (the logo is the kit's own PNG, exact in shape and colour, as `compose_final` draws it), then `box_print.py` for a boxed product, then `upload_link` kind repair. (`--sharp x0,y0,x1,y1` keeps a box crisp in the soften and lens steps when a picture already holds a logo; the tone, halation and grain steps still run there.)
- **Strength.** The defaults (`--strength 1`) are tuned by eye at full size: the first try (grain 0.030, saturation trim 0.18, vignette 0.10) looked like sandpaper and turned a yellow label olive. Keep it subtle; `--no-<step>` turns one step off.
- **Prompts.** The render prompts name the look's camera and light (§7.2) and keep the no-CG style line (`render-checklist.md` §4), so the code finish works on a picture that already reads as a photograph. The W47 Oatside prompts also named the camera body, lens, f-stop and ISO; the owner's verdict on those finals shows whether that helps.

- **Logo place.** The logo can head the text column instead of the top centre (`--logo-x` = the column's left edge, the logo top a little above the headline), so the logo, headline and subline read as one lockup and the logo does not look like an afterthought (owner, W47 Oatside tof, 16-11-2026). A centred layout keeps the logo at the top centre. `headline.py` centres the logo's wordmark, not the whole file: a small mark set apart at its right top (TM, R) is left out of the centre, or the logo leans left (owner, W47 IRVINS: "the logo's leaning too much to the left", 16-11-2026).

The owner saw the W47 Oatside drafts (batch e8d74f93) with the finish and a without/with crop: "i like it! tune it however u see fitting ... overall it looks much more less ai image looking at first glance" (16-11-2026). It is the standard; tune the strength by eye per picture.

### 7.8 Engine 3: drafts start from the picked rough (owner, 19-11-2026)

On the IRVINS engine 3 test (batch 44b748e2) the owner picked 3 concepts from their roughs. The drafts were then built again from new collage skeletons (food shapes cut from a texture photo, the pouch forced to an exact outline) and long prompts of rules (about 2,100 characters). All 7 drafts looked worse than the roughs: "simply using the rough output ... would have suffice ... u've somehow made everything worse than before". The model copied the collage literally, so the pieces looked pasted and the pouch looked CG. More control made the pictures look more like AI, not less. The rough of the same concept, made again at high quality, was the one he approved ("honestly looks great to me. its usable, good to become final").

- **The rough is the start.** When the owner picks a concept from its rough, each draft edits that rough: the rough's URL is the first reference, then the brand's real photos (food, pack: §7.9). The draft's `fromRough` in `direction_save` names the rough (its id is in the key, `renders/<id>/1.jpg`). No new collage skeleton.
- **Change only what is asked.** Each draft keeps the rough's scene and changes only what its question asks: the owner's fix, the room for the words, a crop or the light. The drafts stay close to the rough; `direction_save` gives no diversity warning when all 3 edit the rough.
- **Short prompts.** "Make this photograph again: the same composition, camera, light and positions as image 1. Change only: ..." then the product truth in one or two sentences and the exclusions. Keep it under about 900 characters.
- **High quality for the pick.** The owner's pick is made at high quality (kind `final`) with the picked draft first and the brand's real photos as the truth references.
- **No rough.** A concept with no rough starts from a plan made from real whole-product photos only (§7.4 A, §7.9), with a simple scene of few large objects. A glossy or rounded product (a bottle, a tube, a glossy jar) has no plan: it starts from the brand's real product photo as a reference only (§7.11).

### 7.9 The brand's real photos for food and pack size (owner, 19-11-2026)

The owner: "try to find the real food, the same way u did for the cake slices", and the pack "has to match the actual real life packaging in size, in all dimensions". The size guide is the brand's own reference photos, never an earlier render of ours ("u shouldnt use once you start as size guide, use the actual reference images from the brand's photo").

- **Find the real food first.** Before a draft, look for the brand's own photos of the food on its product pages and site (a cut-out of one piece, a pile, a plated shot; IRVINS: `sefs_chip.png` and the pile of chips on the product page). Upload each with `upload_link` kind `ref` and give it as a reference: "every piece looks exactly like images 2 and 3". This is the same rule as the real cake slices (§7.4, art director step 4a). A food prop that is not the brand's own food (Allies e3: a chilli padi) needs a real photo too: find one with a free licence (for example Wikimedia Commons), crop it close, upload it with `upload_link` kind `ref` and give it as a reference only ("copy the chilli in image 2: its size, slim shape, creases and matte skin"). Keep the prop small and simple. When no real photo exists, the critic flags the concept and the prop is left out (owner, 20-11-2026: the model-drawn chilli padi was "ai slop food").
- **Real proportions of a cut food (owner, 21-11-2026).** On the Cat & the Fiddle engine 3 test the slices came out at invented sizes: too tall, too long or pulled apart, not the real cake's compact straight-cut slices. Before the direction, find the real proportions of each cut food the ad shows: the slice's height against its width and length, the base, and the layers in the cut face. When the brand's own photos show one angle only (a top view), measure the other angles from a retailer's listing or a public review photo of the same product. Save each measure with its source in the kit notes (`brand_save`), and the art director names it in the direction's production risk. A photo that is not the brand's own is for measuring only: give it as a render reference only when its licence allows it.
- **Never cut food shapes out of a texture photo.** Shapes cut from a plate photo are pieces that do not exist; the model copies them and the food looks pasted (IRVINS tally strips).
- **Pack size from the brand.** The pack's proportions come from the brand's packshot and its own product photos. After each draft and final, run `python3 scripts/pack_check.py --render <raw> --at X,Y --bg <stage RRGGBB> --packshot <cut-out packshot>` and save its height over width as `measured` in the draft check (tolerance 0.02). Add `--sideways` for a pack that lies on its side. Check the printed pixel size against the pack in the picture: a thick band of stage colour across the pack splits it. A fold or a crop changes the height: say so, and judge the print too (a round badge must stay round). On the IRVINS test the rolled pouch measured 11.6% short; the approved final with one fold, about 7% short.
- **Simple scenes.** Few large real objects (3 slices and a daisy) read real; a dozen small pieces placed by hand read as a paste-up.

### 7.10 Real-world handling (owner, 19-11-2026)

Every pack and food in the picture must be something a person could do or find in real life.

- **One fold.** A pouch folds at most once, above its print: there is little film above the logo. A fold, clip or hand never covers the brand name, the logo or the product name. A clip that the real pack does not need (a resealable pouch) is fine when the idea needs it.
- **Real food shapes.** A concept that needs food in a shape it never has (long straight fish skin strips) fails at the concept: the critic and the art director say so before any render.
- **No model-drawn side food.** Rice, sauce or a dish beside the product gives the picture away (IRVINS egg bowl). Use only food the brand has real photos of, or leave it out.

### 7.11 Engine 3: the product photographed in the scene light (owner, 20-11-2026)

On the Allies of Skin engine 3 test (batch `f7dbab73`, a glossy cobalt serum bottle) all 7 drafts from product-lock skeletons with the print put back (§7.4 A and F) failed: the bottle kept the packshot's own studio highlight band in every scene, "no matter what angle and or shadow direction it is", so it looked pasted. The remake gave the model the brand's real product photo as a reference only, with no skeleton and no reprint, and described how the scene light falls on the bottle. The owner: "great work on resolving the issue, make sure it doesn't have such an issue the next time round". He picked a remake for its light: "the bright light reflecting on the mirror prop ... the depth of field, the lighting, the shadow the everything seems photographed by a real world camera at the exact angle ... and the writings on the product itself is legible".

- **Glossy or rounded products: reference only.** For a bottle, a tube, a glossy jar or any pack whose surface shows the light, do not paste packshot pixels into the draft (no skeleton, no swap) and do not reprint them over the render. Give the brand's real product photo as a reference: a photo in real light is better than a flat studio cut-out (Allies: the brand's hard-light photo of the bottle and box). The prompt says where the scene light comes from and what it does to the product: the highlight side, the shade side, what the glossy surface reflects (the backdrop, a prop), and the contact shadow on the same side as the other shadows. Ask for the label "exactly as in the reference, every word legible". In `direction_save`, such a draft gives the photo's https URL as `productRef` in place of a `plan`.
- **Flat printed faces keep §7.4.** A skeleton and `reprint` stay for a flat printed face on a matte pack (a carton, a matte pouch) and for engine 2. Even then, check D10 (render checklist): a pasted face must take the scene's light.
- **Check the light on every picture.** Before a draft or a final goes to the owner, compare the product light with the scene light (render checklist D10): the highlight on the light's side, the shade on the far side, reflections from the scene, a contact shadow that agrees. Check the print at full size: a draft with smeared print is made again, not reprinted.
- **A liked layout stays.** When the owner likes the words, the logo or the layout of a draft and asks for a redo for another reason, the redo keeps that layout: the same headline area, logo place and type size, and the product in the same place and at the same size. Only the thing he asked for changes. Give the product top as a percentage of the height in the prompt, and run `headline.py` with the same `--area`, `--logo-top` and `--logo-width`. Allies idea 5: he liked the round 1 words and logo ("already looks great"); the remake moved the bottle and changed the layout, and he passed: "right now everything's ruined".
- **Pack options stay as picked.** When a product has options (cap or pump, a size, a flavour), use the one the owner picked. When he leaves it open, use the brand's main packshot, say on the board which option you used, and never switch between rounds without asking. Save his choice as a verdict note (`batch_update` `ownerVerdicts`, mark `fix`, his words), so `taste_log` gives it to the next batch of that brand. Allies: the cap, not the pump ("cap looks okay and more premium"); the pump was used only because his reference ads show it.
- **Room for the words.** Say in the prompt where the product top sits (for example "the cap top at 40% of the frame height"). When a good render puts the product a little too high, widen the frame in code rather than render again: stretch the plain backdrop band at the top, extend the plain side strips, and scale the picture back to 1024 x 1280. Do this only when the top band and the sides are plain backdrop, and check the seams at full size.

### 7.12 Real size, and measure the final again (owner, 20-11-2026)

On the Allies of Skin test the owner rejected or held back two finals that the drafts had got right. - **Find the real size first.** Before the first image, find the product's real height and width: the product page, a retailer's listing, or the brand's photo of the product beside its carton or in a hand, measured in pixels. Allies serum: 13.5 x 3.6 cm, from the brand's photo of the bottle beside its carton (13.7 x 4 x 4 cm on the Amazon listing). Save it with its source in the kit notes (`brand_save`). For a natural prop, read its real part sizes (a touch-me-not leaflet is 0.7 to 1.2 cm, its flower puff about 2 cm).
- **Give the scale through the picture.** The model ignores centimetres. Tie each prop to something it can see: "the pot rim level with the bottle's shoulder", "each leaflet about as long as the word ECTOIN on the print". Keep the product at its real size and draw the prop true to it; never shrink the product to a guessed prop size.
- **Measure the final again.** After each final, run `python3 scripts/cap_check.py --render <raw final> --box X0,Y0,X1,Y1 --split Y` on a bottle, a jar with a lid or any pack with a cap (the box round the product on plain stage, missing any prop and ending at the base, above the contact shadow; `--split` is the first row below the cap and the neck, read off the picture: the top of any shoulder, because the shoulder belongs to the body). Check the shape (F1) and the print at the size the owner sees (render checklist D11).

The owner approved the W47 v3 Meta text and rejected the rewrite in packs v4 and v5 ("the previously v3 copywriting's much much much better").
