# Taste seed

The owner's taste, copied from v1 `docs/agent-handoff/01-owner-and-intent.md` §5 and `04-evaluation-and-grading.md` §6 (read 2026-11-06). These are his words and marks on v1 ads (batch ids such as B41 are v1 batches). Use this file to seed the first taste canon. Do not turn these lines into code rules.

## 1. What han loves and hates in ads (his words and marks)

**Headlines carry the ad.** han, 2026-10-21: headlines are "extremely crucial critical" to the overall ad. A good headline does not save a weak image either (B45).

Loves (Use, praise):

- A headline that **stops the scroll and sends the eye to the product with curiosity**. A buyer's own words used word for word ("Singaporean exclusive products.", a Reddit thread title, B41). An oddly specific true detail ("Packing for Scotland. Kaya comes too.").
- Headlines he wrote or picked: "Not the neighbourhood bakery this year." (B43), "One cake, every favourite." (Nemotron batch edit), "Chilli crab, no shells to crack." (B47 re-judge), "No fight no squabble." (HFM-muse, Used).
- A **clever object that IS the headline's idea**: B19 butter on toast, B19 zested lemon, B15 empty snail shell for "The snail had a good run." (rated 9.8/10). The Oatside carton casting the shadow of a steaming cup: "clever and good looking" (Use).
- **Calm** ads: one focal product, little to look at (B41 used 4/4 "because they weren't chaotic or busy like B39").
- A **true supporting tagline under the headline** when the product page backs it ("No added preservatives", "Hand-packaged to order"). Without it, ads felt incomplete (B43, B45).
- A real serving **only when it shows what the pack hides** (the Kele tart inside the tin, the kueh lapis layers) or the size of a food sold by weight.
- Props that match the brand's level (a fine Chinese teacup for a heritage brand, B43).

Hates (Pass, "rejected instantly"):

- **AI slop**: a drip on a cup ("very ai slop looking less premium", B44), painted prepared food, a doubled or flat-printed product, garbled letters.
- **A wrong or garbled logo, or a logo outside the safe zone: that alone rejects the ad** (HFB5b "THEI", DeepSeek favicon).
- **Props that add no meaning**: a spare bowl, spoon, plate, blanket, saucer, ice tray, glass of water; an upturned bowl (reads unlucky). One filler prop turns a good ad into a reject (HFM-muse ad 1: "remove it and it becomes an usable ad").
- **A pointer or callout on the wrong part** (Red Velvet label on the blueberry slice). Line-and-dot callouts are optional; a wrong one sinks the ad.
- **Filler stickers and taglines**: shelf life, thin review counts, a rating line that adds nothing, a "useless 15% off tagline sticker", wordy or long taglines.
- An **offer sticker on a ToF ad**. A BoF ad with **no reason to buy now**, or a price that is wrong.
- **Generic slogans, average or pointless headlines**, "boring, non-creative concepts".
- **A "moment" that is not true to Singapore life**: "Baking lapis this CNY? Not again." failed because nobody in Singapore bakes kueh lapis at home for CNY (they buy it).
- **Anything that can read as racist or names an ethnicity**: "The cake my Malay neighbour asks for." → "really really bad headline, sounds racist" (HFX-mix2, 2026-10-25). Treat ethnicity, race and religion in copy as a hard no unless han asks for it (inferred from that note and the HFB6 note "names an ethnicity").
- A headline flavour that is not the slice in front (HFB5b ad 4, HFB6 ad 1).
- Unneeded hands, wrong scale, cheap props (tote, foil, plastic bottle), busy layouts.
- The old "No text below" line (removed 2026-10-21, B41 ad 2). **Never bring it back.** (A 9:16 rule; v2 is 4:5, but the lesson holds: do not shrink a good ad to obey a placement line.)


## 2. Grade the owner gate first (v1 calibration lesson, 2026-10-25)

**han's bar (his words across marks):** creative, thoughtful concepts; headlines that stop the scroll; nothing that adds no meaning; true to Singapore life; never risky about ethnicity, race or religion. Defect-free renders are necessary, not sufficient.

**Grade the "han gate" first, then defects.** For each ad answer, in order:
   1. Concept: is the idea creative and thoughtful, or generic? Would han stop scrolling for this headline? ("boring", "average", "pointless", "generic slogan" = Pass)
   2. Truth and culture: is the moment true of how Singaporeans really behave? Does any line name an ethnicity, race or religion, or carry a negative association (unlucky, rude, stereotyped)? Any yes = **reject**.
   3. Earn its place: does every prop, sticker, tagline and callout add meaning? Is the tagline short and useful? One filler element = Pass.
   4. Logo right and inside the safe zone? Wrong or garbled = reject.
   5. Funnel: ToF with no offer sticker; BoF with a real reason to buy now; a flavour named is the one in front.
   6. Then the render defects (v1 rubric letters A, B, D, E, K, M: premium look, on-brand, text accuracy, safe zone, AI tells, owner defects).
